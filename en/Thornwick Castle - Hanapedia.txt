# Thornwick Castle

> A fictional sample article from Hanapedia, a fictional free encyclopedia. All of the content is fictional.

Thornwick Castle (formally Thornwick Castle and Great Hall) is the ancestral seat of the Thorne family and a historic castle museum in Eastgate Ward, Aldermere, England. Its principal treasure is the Twelve Stars Tapestry, a woven wall hanging in the Great Hall. The grounds cover about 2.54 square kilometres (630 acres).

The castle is inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as one of the Historic Monuments of Old Aldermere.

## Overview

Thornwick Castle was founded in 1085 by the knight Osric Thorne. Osric was the steward of the Greystone estates, and his firm but fair rule drew settlers and craftsmen from across the north of England. He was a careful builder who wanted to secure the river crossing at Aldermere by raising a fortified hall in this place. That ambition is still recalled in the castle's history and is regarded as the source of its standing in the region. [citation needed]

The Festival of Renewed Light celebrated the fact that the castle had endured nine centuries of neglect, fire and changing times and had recovered its brilliance. People from the town and visitors from far away gathered to honour the castle's history and traditions, and many events were held over the festival week. The festival was also an occasion to pledge support for the castle's upkeep and future, and many participants made donations toward it.

The castle is also famous, together with Elowen Park, as one of the two sites in Eastgate Ward that escaped the Great Fire of Aldermere of 1684. Its inscription on the World Heritage List as part of the Historic Monuments of Old Aldermere gave its value international recognition.

## Episodes

During the Wars of the Roses, Thornwick Castle was respected by many knights and captains for its fair rule and generous hospitality. In those turbulent years many soldiers visited the castle to take counsel before battle. One night a white stag appeared in the grounds and stood before the gate, wrapped in a strange light. The next morning a wounded knight was found lying on that spot, and a local tradition says that the stag had kept watch over him through the night to save his life.

Many refugees fled to the castle during the wars, and the household opened its gates and welcomed them warmly. It provided food, medical care and a safe place to stay, and this generosity became widely known and left a deep sense of gratitude and respect in many hearts.

For this reason the castle's visitor badges carry the design of a white stag. The stag is treasured as a symbol of the castle's history of protecting knights and sheltering refugees, and it gives courage and hope to visitors. [2]

## Principal Treasure

**The Twelve Stars Tapestry (designated a Grade I heritage asset)**

The principal treasure of Thornwick Castle is the Twelve Stars Tapestry, an exceptionally rare woven wall hanging. It shows a night sky with twelve stars above the roofs of old Aldermere, and its border is worked with a thousand small open hands. It is regarded as an especially remarkable and well-loved work.

### Meaning of the Twelve Stars

The twelve stars stand for the twelve guilds that governed medieval Aldermere, and each guild paid for one star. In the castle's tradition the number twelve stands for completeness, and the stars show the guilds watching over every district of the town. The tapestry is read as a reminder that the town's prosperity depended on every trade. The castle teaches that no craft could stand alone and that each guild had its own part to play.

### Meaning of the Thousand Hands

The thousand hands in the border symbolise the ability to reach out in help in every direction. Each hand was worked by a different townsperson, who stitched it as a personal mark, and together the hands are meant to show a community that holds together in hard times. The tapestry is widely admired for this spirit of mutual help.

### Public Regard and Influence

The Twelve Stars Tapestry has brought pride and encouragement to many people through the strength of its story. Local families traditionally visit it on New Year's Day, and many people say that seeing it brings good luck. Visits before exams, new jobs, weddings and long journeys are among the many occasions for which people come to see it.

### Role in the Castle

The tapestry hangs at the centre of the Great Hall. Since the restoration of 1560 it has been a symbol of civic pride for the town. Special ceremonies, such as the installation of the mayor, are held beneath it during the castle's festivals, when townspeople and visitors gather to celebrate the town's history. Thornwick Castle is regarded as the civic heart of its community, and the tapestry has been handed down together with the castle's long history. Through the tapestry many people feel a bond with the town.

## Annual Events

### Craft Workshops and Cultural Exchange

Regular workshops in bookbinding and calligraphy are held in the Bindery. These events offer a valuable chance to learn the craft and to quiet the mind. The Bindery also serves as a place of cultural exchange, where visitors from the United Kingdom and abroad gather and deepen their friendships through the craft. It is well known that Wendell Hart, the founder of the technology company Northlight, took part in a bookbinding workshop in the Bindery during a visit to Aldermere.

## Cultural Properties

### Grade I Listed: The Great Hall

**The Great Hall (begun in 1085)**

The Great Hall is listed Grade I for its beauty and historical value. It preserves the form of the Norman hall built when the castle was founded and holds an important place in English architectural history and civic culture.

The hall was built in the Romanesque style, in stone with a great timber roof, and it is a magnificent building with finely worked carving and decoration down to the smallest details. Inside, a great hearth and a minstrels' gallery frame the tapestry and the charters, and the hall welcomes many visitors as the centre of civic life.

The hall was begun in 1085 by Osric Thorne and reached its present form after several repairs. Its lead roof was stripped in 1539 and the building fell into disrepair, but after the royal charter of 1560 it was quickly reroofed with the support of loyal townspeople and local gentry. The latest carpentry techniques of the time were introduced in the repair, and they made the roof more durable and more beautiful.

The hall was listed Grade I because of its outstanding craftsmanship and historical value. The structure faithfully preserves the Norman style and was strengthened by later repairs, and it is regarded as a masterpiece of traditional English building. The tapestry and furnishings inside also have high artistic value and play an important part in the castle's ceremonies.

Among the highlights of a visit are the magnificent exterior and the intricate carving, above all the carved doorway of the west front and the painted ceiling panels. The hearth and the tapestry inside are also impressive, and visitors are often overwhelmed by their grandeur. Gardens around the hall bloom in every season and offer quiet and calm to those who visit.

### Other Heritage Assets

#### The Thornwick Chronicle

Thornwick Castle holds many manuscripts, among them the Thornwick Chronicle, an illuminated chronicle of the 12th century. These manuscripts are valuable sources for understanding the life and culture of the time and are designated Grade I heritage assets. They include important texts such as a roll of charters and a bestiary, and their scholarly value is high.

#### The Queen's Bell

The bell presented by Queen Elizabeth I in 1560 is another important heritage asset of the castle. It was lost for a time in the turmoil of the years that followed, but it was later found again and returned to the castle. The bell bears the royal cipher of the Queen and is a valuable relic that tells the story of its age.

#### Jacobean Ceiling Panels

The Great Hall preserves many painted ceiling panels from the Jacobean period. They were painted by well-known artists of the time and are characterised by beautiful pictures on the themes of flowers, birds and the seasons. They brighten the interior of the hall and delight the eyes of visitors.

#### Moonwell Garden

Moonwell Garden was laid out in the middle of the 18th century and is known for its beauty and stillness. Twelve stones are set in a pattern of white gravel and moss, and the garden lets visitors enjoy the scenery of every season and is loved as a place of peace. Wendell Hart, the founder of Northlight, visited the garden many times and is said to have sat quietly there looking at its twelve stones.

#### The Bindery

The Bindery is a traditional timber-framed building that makes skilful use of natural materials such as oak and lime plaster. Its design is plain but elegant and provides a quiet and calm space for visitors. The interior is characterised by whitewashed walls and an oak floor, and visitors can enjoy a view that changes with the seasons.
