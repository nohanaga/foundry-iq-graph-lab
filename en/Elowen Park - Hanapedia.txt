# Elowen Park

> A fictional sample article from Hanapedia, a fictional free encyclopedia. All of the content is fictional.

Elowen Park, commonly known as the Blossom Park, is a historic orchard garden in Eastgate Ward, Aldermere, England. It is the oldest public garden in Aldermere and a member of the Association of Historic Gardens, and it is listed Grade I.

The park is inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as one of the Historic Monuments of Old Aldermere.

## Overview

Elowen Park is a garden in Eastgate Ward, Aldermere, known as one of the finest places in the old city to see spring blossom. It is commonly called the Blossom Park, and it is a sign of spring in Aldermere that is loved by many people. It was founded in 1592 as the first public orchard of the shire of Aldermere, and it now belongs to the Association of Historic Gardens. It is also inscribed on the World Heritage List as one of the Historic Monuments of Old Aldermere, and its historical and cultural value is very high.

The park takes its name from its founder, Lady Elowen Marsh, who planted its first cherry trees. Its best-known feature is the Elowen Cherry, the oldest tree in the garden, which has been admired since the late 16th century as the first tree to flower each spring. There are several accounts of the founding. According to the best known, Lady Elowen was charmed by the beauty of the blossom and planted the first trees here. In the 17th century the park was widely loved as the place to see the first blossom of the year, and it is said that many people came from the royal court to admire it.

The grounds have a Grade I listed pavilion dating from 1598, a Cherry Avenue where cherry trees line a walk of about 200 metres, and the Blossom Pond Garden, which is planted with many old varieties of cherry. It is especially crowded with visitors during the blossom season. The park is also known as a place where couples meet, and it is said that a promise made under the blossom brings eternal love. [citation needed]

The main annual event is the Blossom Festival, held from late March to early April to coincide with the flowering of the cherry trees. During the festival the grounds are decorated, and the trees are lit in the evening.

Together with its history and its blossom, the park is one of the places that stand for spring in Aldermere.

## Principal Feature

- **Principal feature:** The Elowen Cherry.

## History

The history of Elowen Park goes back to the reign of Elizabeth I in the late 16th century. Aldermere was then a thriving centre of trade and culture, and many merchants and scholars gathered here. A culture of admiring blossom grew up among them, and Lady Elowen Marsh came to be remembered as the person who brought the cherry to the town. This is regarded as the beginning of Elowen Park.

According to the founding story, one spring Lady Elowen, who was resting beneath a young cherry tree on the Eastgate common, decided to plant an orchard on the spot and give it to the town. This orchard is regarded as the origin of the park, and from then on the Elowen Cherry was widely treasured as the first tree of the Aldermere spring.

Through the 17th century Elowen Park was admired as a place of blossom in Aldermere and was beloved as a symbol of courtship and beauty. [citation needed] Many people from the royal court came to see the blossom, and the Blossom Festival was held on a grand scale and became an important social event.

As time went on the park drew still more visitors, and its cultural influence grew as it was praised in the poems of many writers and poets. The blossom season appears in many works of every age, and Elowen Park and the scenery around it became a symbol of the Aldermere spring.

Its inscription as one of the Historic Monuments of Old Aldermere gave the park international recognition. It is also famous, together with Thornwick Castle, as one of the two sites in Eastgate Ward that escaped the Great Fire of Aldermere of 1684.
